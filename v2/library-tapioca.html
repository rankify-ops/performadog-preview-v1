Tapioca

Tapioca as a grain-free carbohydrate in pet food: its nutrients, digestibility and why dogs aren't naturally allergic to grains or legumes.

2019 · 3 min read

What Is Tapioca?

Tapioca is a starchy vegetable, extracted from the root of the Cassava plant. If you’ve never heard of Cassava before, it is because it’s found in South America and is not commonly seen in Europe or North America.

The reason Tapioca is an exciting ingredient in dog food is that it is being used more and more in premium foods as an alternative to low-quality grains or common ingredients that often cause allergies. If your dog has a problem with grains, then Tapioca may be an option alongside other starches like Potatoes and Sweet Potatoes.

Tapioca – Nutrition

  • Free of common allergens.
  • Cholesterol-free.
  • Has dietary fibre.
  • Easy to digest.
  • Supports weight gain.
  • Source of calcium.
  • Low in sodium.
  • Source of folate.

The high amount of carbohydrate in tapioca is effective in helping gain some healthy weight. Containing the right amount of fibre, tapioca is good for maintaining the health of your digestive system. Prevents iron-deficiency diseases like anemia since it can help the body create red blood cells.

It is present in dog food as a source of complex carbohydrates and as a binding agent. While dogs certainly do not need excessive levels of carbohydrates, a modest sum is perfectly acceptable. For this purpose, the complex carbohydrates provided by Tapioca can fulfil this need with ease.

It does provide some micro-nutrients including a mix of the B Vitamins such as Folate and the Vitamin B6. Tapioca is better known for its mineral content, as Tapioca is an excellent source of iron, manganese, and calcium. These minerals are essential for the production of red blood cells and healthy bones.

Tapioca, like Potatoes and Sweet Potatoes, is a drastically different source of carbohydrates when compared to both grains and legumes, which can allow it to be an excellent option in specialist diets.

An important follow up to this point is for you to understand that dogs are not naturally allergic or intolerant of grains, legumes or white potatoes or any other vegetable ingredient. As a consequence, your dog could likely eat a diet containing any of the mentioned ingredients without any negative effect. While some may argue all grains are inferior quality ingredients, whole grains such as Rice and Oatmeal are reasonable dog food ingredients.

Tapioca As A Binding Agent

A binding agent is an essential component of dry dog food as it is the glue that keeps the kibble pieces uniform and in their desired shape. While realistically the shape and texture of your dog food aren’t incredibly important, consumers are used to aesthetically pleasing small brown pieces, and as a consequence, dog food manufacturers put significant effort into maintaining this appearance.

Should Tapioca Be In Dog Food?

The primary reason that Tapioca is being used more commonly as a dog food ingredient is that it helps create a satisfying and uniform texture and shape. It’s also a dense energy source, and it acts like fibre, which helps with your dog maintain consistent digestion.

The significant benefit is in its use in specialist dog food diets for those with severe allergies or sensitivities. Here’s where tapioca comes into its own. Because most dogs won’t have come across Tapioca in their lifetimes as it’s so unusual, it’s unlikely to trigger an allergic reaction. Potatoes, on the other hand, while a reasonable option for dogs with a grain or legume allergy, is another possible trigger for further undiagnosed food allergies as their use is more widespread and most dogs will have been previously exposed.

If you’re looking to take all of the possible triggers away from your dog’s daily kibble or wet food, then, by all means, choose a grain-free recipe with Tapioca added. Ensure that meat is at the top of the list of ingredients, and that tapioca is not there in place of vegetables that provide essential nutrients.

Is Tapioca The Best Carbohydrate Ingredient For My Dog’s Diet?

If you need to put your dog on a specialist diet, then you should strongly consider a dog food formula that uses Tapioca as its primary carbohydrate source, spending the additional time and money selecting a dog food that contains a high proportion of high-quality meat and or fish ingredients.

These meat and fish ingredients are the most critical aspect of your dog’s diet and provide the easily digested and biologically appropriate protein and fat that your dog so desperately needs for growth, repair, and maintenance.
