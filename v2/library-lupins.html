Sweet Lupin

Sweet lupins as a high-protein, high-fibre legume grown in Australia.

2019 · 1 min read

What Is Lupin?

Lupin or lupini beans are the yellow legume seeds of the genus Lupinus. They are traditionally eaten as a pickled snack food, primarily in the Mediterranean basin and Latin America. The bitter variety of the beans are high in alkaloids and are extremely bitter unless rinsed methodically. Lupin (sometimes called “lupine”) is a legume belonging to the same plant family as peanuts. “For many people, eating lupin or a lupin-derived ingredient, such as flour, is safe,” says Stefano Luccioli, M.D., a senior medical advisor at the Food and Drug Administration (FDA).

What Is Lupin Good For?

We only use sweet lupins, which are white or speckled with brown. They are a good source of fibre and an excellent source of protein. Sweet lupins also contain a moderate amount of polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFA’s) including omega 6 fatty acids, which are the precursor for the production of arachidonic acid in the body. Arachidonic acid plays a significant role in combating inflammation within the body.
